VEP Lab time, frequency and code modulation

frame clock 60 Hz (wall clock, not vsync) live off

A visual evoked potential (VEP) BCI puts several targets on a screen and reads from the EEG which one the user is looking at. Bin, Gao, Wang, Hong and Gao (2009) sort these systems by how the targets are told apart, the way radio channels share one band: by time, by frequency, or by code. The panels below draw each coding's stimulus and its evoked response from one linear toy model, so the figures the paper uses to explain them come out of the same arithmetic the Python demo (python -m brainsync.vep) runs.

t-VEP: time division (TDMA)

Targets take turns. Each square flashes briefly in its own slot and no two flashes ever overlap, so every flash belongs to exactly one target. The brain answers each flash with a short flash VEP; averaging the EEG after one target's own onsets keeps its response and averages the others' out. Slow: the paper keeps flash rates under 4 Hz and needs many flashes per decision.

f-VEP: frequency division (FDMA)

Every target flickers at its own frequency, all at the same time. Above about 6 Hz the responses to consecutive flashes merge into a steady-state VEP at that frequency and its harmonics, so the power spectrum of the EEG peaks at the fixated target's frequency. Simple and training-free, which is why BrainSync's own decoder is one, but the usable frequencies are few and their responses differ in size.

c-VEP: code division (CDMA)

Every target shows the same pseudorandom m-sequence, each delayed by its own lag. An m-sequence is nearly uncorrelated with its own shifts, so the targets evoke the same waveform at different delays, and one template learned on one target, shifted, identifies any of them. The response is broadband, which helps against the EEG's narrow rhythms; it needs a training stage and a sync marker every cycle.

t-VEP four targets, 2-frame flashes in 15-frame slots, shuffled order (tvep_4)

a Stimulus, one second (Fig. 2a)

Rows are targets, cells are frames (60 per second). A round is four slots of 15 frames, one flash each, in an order shuffled every round.

b Evoked response of the fixated target, 2 s

Each flash evokes one short VEP; the response has died out well before the target's next flash.

c One flash response (Fig. 2b)

The model's flash VEP: nothing for 60 ms, then a damped 6 Hz sine. The dashed line is the average of the response after the fixated target's onsets.

Each flash is 33 ms; a flash rate of 1 Hz per target, 4 Hz over the row.

f-VEP six targets at 15, 12, 10, 8.6, 7.5 and 6 Hz, on/off (fvep_6)

a Stimulus, one second (Fig. 3a)

The paper's six frequencies are 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10 frames per cycle at 60 Hz (8.6 Hz is 60/7).

b Evoked response of the fixated target, 2 s

The flash responses overlap into a periodic wave: a steady-state VEP.

c Power spectrum of the response (Fig. 3b)

Narrow peaks at the fundamental and its harmonics; the other targets' frequencies (ticks) carry nothing in this model.

15 Hz is inside the 15–25 Hz photosensitive band.

c-VEP one 63-bit m-sequence from x6 + x + 1, every target a shifted copy

a Stimulus, one second (Fig. 4a)

One cycle is 63 frames (1.05 s). Every row is the row above delayed by the lag.

b Evoked response of the fixated target, 2 s (Fig. 4b)

Irregular but periodic with the cycle; every target's response is this waveform at its own delay.

c Autocorrelation of the m-sequence (Fig. 4d)

N = 63 at lag 0 and −1 at every other lag: the shifts are as close to orthogonal as a binary sequence gets.

d Power spectrum of the response (Fig. 4c)

Broadband, spread over the kernel's passband instead of a few sharp lines.

An m-sequence has energy up to the frame rate, including 15–25 Hz.

Honest note: every response on this page is a linear toy model, the stimulus bits convolved with one made-up flash VEP (brainsync.synthetic.vep_kernel), with no noise, no non-linearity and no peripheral response. Real EEG is none of those things; the page explains the codings, it does not predict how well any of them works on a headset.

Flickering light

The live previews flash squares between dark and light. Flicker at 15–25 Hz is the band most likely to trigger a seizure in people with photosensitive epilepsy, and the m-sequence and the flashes contain that band. Don't continue if you or anyone watching has epilepsy or has had a seizure.

Press Esc at any time to stop the flicker. Nothing flickers until you press a start button.
