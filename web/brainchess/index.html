    How it works
    
    
    
      
    
        
    c-VEP: three codes and a way back
    
        
    The default coding is the BrainSync c-VEP standard. One 63-frame pseudo-random sequence of white and black frames (1.05 s at 60 Hz) runs on every target, each 21 frames (350 ms) later than the one before: group A on the board, group B, and a BACK panel. Before the game the decoder trains on 30 cycles while you look at A, then reads every cycle: under the prompt each target gets a bar and its score, the correlation of your EEG with that target's delayed response. Two decisions in a row for the same target make a pick, about 3.7 s after the board changed. BACK undoes the last pick at every step, so a wrong pick costs one more pick, not the whole move. On the PLAY screen the middle panel, RESTART, starts the move over.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Two frequencies, one cut
    
        
    Only two flicker rates work reliably for this player: 15 Hz and 17 Hz. Every piece you can move flickers at one of them. A straight line between two files or two ranks splits the pieces into a 15 Hz group and a 17 Hz group, so the likely moves weigh about the same on each side.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Keep looking, the board narrows
    
        
    You keep your eyes on your piece. When the decoder names its group, the other group stops flickering and the rest split again. Once one piece is left, the same happens for its destination. Then you confirm: PLAY on the left at 15 Hz, BACK on the right at 17 Hz. BACK starts the move over.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    The fastest pick takes 2.75 s
    
        
    The decoder reads 1.5 s windows every 0.25 s. A window counts only if it starts 0.5 s after the board changed. Four agreeing windows in a row make a pick.

    Picks and time per move come from simulated games against the real decoder settings, confirm step included. Wrong picks and the median time to a correct pick come from replays of real EEG recordings, where the decoder also often gives no answer at all. The replay timing is the decoder's own vote on recorded EEG, not timed from a screen change through the 0.5 s settle gate, so it is not comparable with the 2.75 s minimum above.
    
    
    No EEG runs in your browser. The decoder on this page is simulated: each window names the group you point at as often as the accuracy slider says. Real flicker counts screen frames and uses the refresh rate it measures before it starts. Browser frame timing has not been checked for EEG use.
